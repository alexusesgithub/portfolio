Overview

Decisio is an enterprise decision-intelligence platform: it takes a business's own documents and data, retrieves what's actually relevant to a given question, and uses an LLM to turn that into a specific, grounded recommendation — business diagnosis, competitor analysis, or contextual decision support, depending on the query.

The Problem

Generic LLM advice is easy to get and rarely useful — it doesn't know a business's actual numbers, competitors, or documents. Businesses have the data to answer their own strategic questions, but connecting "here's our data" to "here's what to do about it" is a manual, analyst-driven process. Decisio's premise: retrieve the relevant context first, then let the LLM reason over it — instead of asking the model to reason from nothing.

The system is built as modular pipelines rather than one monolithic prompt — retrieval, context assembly, and generation are separable stages, which made it possible to tune each one (and swap data sources) independently during the hackathon.

Capabilities

  • Business diagnosis — surfaces issues and patterns from a business's own operational data.
  • Competitor analysis — retrieves and compares competitor-related documents and context.
  • Contextual decision support — answers grounded in retrieved documents, not general model knowledge.
  • Interactive dashboards — presents retrieval and recommendation output in a usable interface, not raw text.

Key Challenges

  • Grounding without over-constraining — retrieval needed to be tight enough that the LLM couldn't drift into generic advice, but wide enough to actually answer the question.
  • Building it under hackathon time pressure — modular pipelines were the only way to parallelize retrieval, backend, and dashboard work across the team in the time available.
  • Making recommendations feel specific — the bar for "useful" output was higher than a chatbot: judges could immediately tell generic advice apart from document-grounded advice.

Outcome

Decisio placed in the top 10 among 1,500+ participants, validating the core bet that retrieval-grounded reasoning beats a raw LLM prompt for real business decision support.
