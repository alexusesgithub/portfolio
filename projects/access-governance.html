Overview

Most enterprises run identity and access data across HR systems, IAM tools, and dozens of individual applications — each with its own notion of who a user is and what they can do. Nobody owns a single, reconciled view of it. I designed and built a governance platform that consolidates application, identity, account and entitlement data into one inventory, detects access problems automatically, and gives reviewers a structured workflow to act on them.

This started as a User Access Review (UAR) tool and grew into a broader identity-governance proof of concept covering the full lifecycle: ingestion → reconciliation → investigation → review → remediation → audit.

Transparency note: this is an internal prototype built with sanitized and synthetic demo data. It does not use live production integrations, real employee or customer records, or confidential Sennovate client data. Screens and data shown here are illustrative, not a live system.

The Problem

User Access Reviews are usually done by exporting spreadsheets from five different systems and manually cross-referencing them — a process that's slow, error-prone, and nearly impossible to audit. The specific failure modes I kept seeing:

  • Terminated employees whose application access was never revoked.
  • Service and orphan accounts with no clear human or system owner.
  • The same identity represented inconsistently across multiple source systems.
  • No single place to see evidence for an access decision, let alone prove it during an audit.

On top of that model sits the operational layer: exceptions, UAR reviews, remediations, verifications, certifications, and audit events — each one traceable back to the source data that produced it.

Platform surfaces built on this model:

  • Overview & Triage — a single view of governance health across all connected sources.
  • Exception Center — every detected issue, prioritized and assignable.
  • Identities & Accounts and Applications — the resolved inventory itself.
  • Source Data Explorer and Investigation Drawer — drill into raw source records and compare evidence across systems.
  • UAR Review Console — the reviewer-facing workflow for certifying or revoking access.
  • Remediation & Verification Pipeline and Audit Evidence Matrix — closing the loop and proving it happened.

The Reconciliation Engine

The reconciliation engine is the part that actually finds problems. It walks the resolved data model and flags specific, named exception types rather than a generic "review this" flag:

TERMINATED_ACTIVE_ACCESS

Identity is terminated in HR but still has an active account or entitlement somewhere.

ORPHAN_ACCOUNT

Account exists in a target system with no linked identity at all.

UNLINKED_ACCOUNT

Account exists but couldn't be confidently resolved to a known identity.

DUPLICATE_IDENTITY

The same person appears as more than one identity record across sources.

CONFLICTING_ACCESS_STATUS

Access status disagrees between two source systems for the same identity.

UNOWNED_SERVICE_ACCOUNT

Service account with no assigned human or team owner of record.

Every exception carries the evidence that produced it, so a reviewer never has to take the engine's word for it — they can open the Investigation Drawer and see the conflicting records side by side.

Key Challenges

  • Identity resolution across inconsistent sources — the same person shows up with different names, IDs and formats per system; resolving them into one identity without false-merging distinct people was the hardest single problem.
  • Designing exceptions reviewers would actually trust — a flag with no evidence gets ignored. Every exception had to carry its supporting records.
  • Making remediation provable, not just logged — audit evidence had to be structured so a certification could be reconstructed after the fact, not just asserted.

Outcome

The platform evolved from a single-purpose UAR tool into a working proof of concept for a broader identity-governance product line at Sennovate, with a data model and reconciliation engine that generalizes across new source systems without a rebuild.
